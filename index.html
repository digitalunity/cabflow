Where generic software stops

Fitted-furniture manufacturing becomes specialised exactly where most business software runs out of answers.

Manufacturers do not only manage customers and orders. They carry years of knowledge about product rules, materials, machining, production exceptions, stock movement, dispatch and delivery. When that knowledge lives inside ageing software and workarounds, replacement becomes an operational-risk problem rather than a normal software upgrade.

The product model

The job should mean the same thing in the office, the factory and the van.

Parametric core

Know what you are making.

Product definitions, materials, fittings, variables and rules become explicit operational knowledge rather than behaviour hidden inside one ageing application.

Manufacturing intent

Keep machine detail at the edge.

The manufacturable result stays separate from machine-specific output formats, so the product model is not trapped inside one machine contract.

Operational reality

Model exceptions rather than hide them.

Production, warehouse movement, dispatch and delivery contain real-world exceptions. Those states should be visible and auditable rather than forced into fake jobs and workarounds.

Modernise without the leap of faith

Replace the fragile implementation. Keep the knowledge that makes the factory work.

The difficult part is not choosing a new framework. It is discovering which behaviours are business rules, which are machine contracts, which are customer-specific workarounds and which can finally be retired.

The migration approach is based on evidence, regression fixtures and staged cutover. High-risk calculations and manufacturing outputs are proven before they are trusted with production.
