The gap CabFlow is designed to close

Generic business software stops just where cabinet manufacturing becomes specialised.

Cabinet manufacturers do not only manage customers and orders. They carry years of knowledge about cabinet rules, materials, machining, production exceptions, dispatch and delivery. When that knowledge lives inside ageing software and workarounds, replacing the system is an operational risk, not a normal software upgrade.

Modernisation without the leap of faith

Replace the fragile implementation. Keep the knowledge that makes the factory work.

The hard part of replacing mature manufacturing software is not choosing a new framework. It is discovering which behaviours are business rules, which are machine contracts, which are customer-specific workarounds and which can finally be retired.

CabFlow's migration approach is built around evidence, regression fixtures and staged cutover. High-risk cabinet calculations and manufacturing outputs are proven before they are trusted with production.

Designed for factory reality

Cloud where it helps. Local and offline where the work demands it.

Machine-specific without machine-bound

Cabinet and job logic should describe what must be manufactured. Output adapters can then handle the format required by a particular saw, optimiser or downstream machine.

Local boundaries for factory systems

Customer-site agents and relays can bridge private machines and local resources without turning factory databases or file shares into public cloud dependencies.

Delivery that survives lost signal

Field workflows are being shaped around planned runs, physical units, scanning and auditable exceptions, with offline operation treated as a real requirement rather than an edge case.
